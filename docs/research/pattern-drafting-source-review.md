## Findings

### ISO body measurement definitions

**Identified standard:** ISO 8559-1:2017, *Size designation of clothes — Part 1: Anthropometric definitions for body measurement*, first edition, published 2017-03. ISO's official record gives an 80-page document, identifies ISO/TC 133, and says the publication was reviewed and confirmed in 2026 and remains current. Its official abstract says it describes anthropometric measurements as a basis for physical and digital anthropometric databases, guides clothing practitioners on selecting population segments and creating size/shape profiles, and gives guidance on taking measurements and their anatomical/anthropometrical basis. The abstract also mentions Annex A's clothing-size pictogram. [ISO 8559-1:2017 official catalogue record](https://www.iso.org/standard/61686.html) (abstract; General information; life-cycle status).

**Digitally verified preview content:** The ISO-hosted reader rendered the Foreword, Introduction, and Clause 1 (Scope). The Introduction describes Part 1 as defining/generating anthropometric measurements for clothing size/shape profiles and refers to ISO 7250-1 for measurements and procedures. Clause 1 says the measurements can form a basis for anthropometric databases and guide clothing practitioners in selecting market segments and creating size/shape profiles; it also says the list guides how to take measurements. The contents tree confirms these relevant headings (headings only, not the provisions): Clause 3.1 “Landmark points and levels”; 3.2 “Lines and planes”; Clause 4 “Measuring conditions and equipment,” with 4.1 conditions/apparatus and 4.2 equipment; and Clause 5 “Basic body measurements,” including vertical measurements (5.1), breadths/widths/depths (5.2), girths (5.3), and body-surface distances (5.4). In 5.3, headings include bust girth (5.3.4), contoured bust girth (5.3.5), chest girth at axilla (5.3.6), upper-chest girth (5.3.7), under-bust girth (5.3.8), and waist girth (5.3.10). In 5.4, headings include shoulder length (5.4.1), across-back and across-front widths (5.4.3, 5.4.4, 5.4.7), back/front neck point to waist (5.4.5, 5.4.8), scye-depth length (5.4.6), and side-neck-point to bust-point (5.4.10). Annex C is titled “Landmark and measurement mapping.” These TOC labels establish that the standard contains entries on those topics; they do not establish their definitions or construction landmarks. [ISO 8559-1:2017 official reader/catalogue](https://www.iso.org/standard/61686.html), Foreword, Introduction, Clause 1, and contents headings for Clauses 3–5/Annex C.

**Still not digitally verified:** The preview navigation exposed the relevant clause headings, but exact entry text for landmarks, measurement routes, posture/equipment requirements, tables, figures, and page references was not retrievable in this review. No specific anatomical landmark or measurement path is therefore asserted from ISO. A heading such as “bust girth” does not by itself verify how the tape is placed or which landmarks define it. The official reader appeared to navigate to Clause 3.1, but its content pane remained on the Foreword; the entries could not be opened reliably through the available preview interaction. The ISO OBP landing page describes preview/search functionality and presents a terms/anonymous-access gate. [ISO OBP](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui/en/#iso:std:iso:8559:-1:ed-1:v1:en).

**Part 2 status and revision relation (verified):** ISO's dedicated catalogue record identifies **ISO 8559-2:2025**, *Size designation of clothes — Part 2: Primary and secondary dimension indicators*, as Edition 2, published 2025-09 (stage 60.60), 41 pages, and Published. Its lifecycle explicitly lists ISO 8559-2:2017 as Previously / Withdrawn. The 2017 record independently labels itself Withdrawn and lists the 2025 edition under “Revised by.” This establishes that the 2025 edition revised/superseded the 2017 edition. The 2025 catalogue abstract available in English in the prior catalogue search says it specifies primary and secondary measurements for specified garment types to establish a body-measurement-based size designation system; the current dedicated page's abstract is displayed in French in this browser retrieval. Neither record's abstract is full-text verification. Part 2 concerns size designation, not a bodice-block drafting method. [ISO 8559-2:2025 official record](https://www.iso.org/standard/85590.html); [ISO 8559-2:2017 withdrawn record](https://www.iso.org/standard/64075.html).

### Ease terminology and amounts

The publisher-hosted Chapter 1 sample (printed p. 13) contains qualitative block-function ease discussion and dress/jacket and close-/easy-fitting block contrasts, as summarized above. It gives **no numeric ease amount or distribution on that page** and does not explicitly establish “functional/wearing ease” versus “design/style ease.” Since the sample is not tied by publisher evidence to the target sixth edition, these are not sixth-edition findings. ISO 8559-1's catalogue abstract concerns body measurements, not garment ease. ISO 8559-2:2017's official abstract says garment measurements/allowances are chosen by designers/manufacturers for wear, style, cut, and fashion elements; however, the 2017 edition is withdrawn and this is not a numeric ease rule. No numeric ease/drafting value is sourced or recommended here.

Therefore, no ease amount, minimum, proportional allowance, or per-measurement distribution is verified. Do not treat commonly repeated bust-ease values or distinctions as authoritative without checking an identified, relevant primary source. An appropriate primary authority may be the exact Aldrich sixth-edition chapter if it explicitly addresses ease, or a directly accessible standard/textbook whose scope and terminology match the intended garment and fabric; record the edition, page/figure, wording, and context before adopting a rule.

## Manual verification checklist

1. Obtain legitimate access to current ISO 8559-1:2017 full text. Inspect Clause 3 (landmarks/planes), Clause 4 (conditions/equipment), selected Clause 5 entries (especially 5.3/5.4), Annex B posture illustrations, and Annex C mapping. Record exact definitions, landmarks, path/plane, posture, and clause/table/figure/page. Distinguish body measurements from garment measurements and pattern dimensions. The preview headings alone do not authorize a measurement definition.
2. ISO 8559-2:2025's publication and revision of withdrawn ISO 8559-2:2017 are verified from both dedicated catalogue records. If standardized size designation is needed, obtain the 2025 full text; do not use Part 2 as a drafting recipe.
3. Resolve the Wiley sample identity with first-party evidence tying a legitimate sample/title leaf to sixth-edition ISBN 9781119028284. Only then inspect the target edition's bodice-block construction chapter and relevant fit/ease text. Record exact body-measurement basis, construction steps, diagrams, formulas, assumptions, fit and ease, with verified printed pages. The local TOC sheet is not edition-authenticated and its page references should not be treated as verified target-edition pagination.
4. For ease, require source-owner wording and context: distinguish block/functional or wearing ease from design/style ease, and establish garment type, fabric/stretch, fit intent, measurement axis, and whether quantities are total circumference or a pattern fraction. If no suitable primary source is accessible, leave the issue unresolved; label any future project choice as a design decision, not an ISO/Aldrich fact.

## Safe implications for a future narrow implementation spec

- Treat ISO 8559-1 as a candidate authority for **body-measurement definitions and measurement procedure**, not as a verified source for drafting geometry or ease. First obtain and cite the exact relevant entries and landmarks.
- Treat Aldrich sixth edition as the intended candidate for a particular basic darted bodice block only after resolving the sample identity and checking its actual construction pages. Current extract chapter headings do not prove that it is the sixth edition or establish the block algorithm.
- Keep input measurements, garment/block dimensions, functional ease, design ease, and seam allowance conceptually separate. This is a scoping recommendation, not a claim that the cited sources prescribe a particular data model or arithmetic.
- A bounded spec should name the exact block/version, required measurements and their source definitions, drafting steps/rules with exact references, fit/fabric assumptions, and all ease amounts and distribution only when verified. Otherwise explicitly leave those inputs/rules undefined and block implementation of them.
- No geometry should be implemented from this report alone. ISO's scope and relevant measurement-clause headings are confirmed, but operative ISO definitions/landmarks, Aldrich construction steps, and any sixth-edition-specific or numeric ease rules remain unverified and require exact-source full-text inspection. The accessible Wiley-hosted text only supports qualitative, edition-unattributed ease discussion.
